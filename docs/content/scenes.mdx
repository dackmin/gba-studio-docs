# Scenes

Scenes in GBA Studio (just like in GB Studio) define the type of game you make.

<img src="/images/scene-props.png" alt="Scene Properties" className="rounded-xl" width={300} />

## Scene Types

Here are the different scene types you can choose from:
- **Logos** (default): A scene typically used to display logos or introductory screens at the start
or end of the game.
- **Top-Down**: A top-down view scene, typically used for RPGs or simple grid based games
(like classic Pokémon games).
- **Adventure**: Same as top-down, but typically used for adventure games where movements are pixel
based (like old Zelda games).
- **Side-Scroller**: A side-scrolling view scene, typically used for platformer games (like classic
Mario or Sonic games).
- **Point & Click**: Instead of controlling a character directly, the player is a cursor that
interacts with the environment by clicking on objects or locations (like Monkey Island).

:::tip
Games can have multiple scenes of different types, allowing you to mix and match various gameplay
styles within the same game.
Feel free to experiment and add a splash of razzle-dazzle!
:::

## Backgrounds

Backgrounds in GBA Studio define the size and the visual appearance of your scene.
They are imported in the Assets panel and have various restrictions & limitations, see the
[Backgrounds documentation](/docs/assets/backgrounds) for more details.

<img src="/images/scene-background.png" alt="Scene Backgrounds" className="rounded-xl" height={300} />

<br /><br />

:::warning
When changing the scene's background, collisions won't be automatically removed, but will not
correspond to the new background's layout either.
Please make sure to adjust the collisions manually after changing the background to match the
new layout.
:::

## HUD

The HUD (Heads-Up Display) in GBA Studio is used to display important information to the player,
such as health, score, or other runtime-specific data.
See the [HUD documentation](/docs/content/hud) for more details.

# Adding Content

Now that you've created an empty scene with just a dialog in it, you can start adding more content
to it.

## Adding a Background

You can add a background to your scene by selecting `File > Import > Background` from the menu
(or going to the `Images` tab and clicking the `+` button right to `Backgrounds`) and choosing an
image file from your computer.

:::tip
If you don't have one, you can download [this one](/images/map_manor.bmp) I made for one of my own
games.
:::

Backgrounds have a few requirements (see the [Backgrounds documentation](/docs/content/backgrounds)
for more details):
- They must be at least 256x256 pixels in size.
- They should have a maximum of ~1000 tiles (a tile is an 8x8 pixel block of pixels, counted the
same in every direction).

<img src="/images/background-import.png" alt="Import Background" className="rounded-xl" height={400} />

<br /><br />

Once imported, you can assign it to your scene through the Right Sidebar under the background
property.

:::warning
Big backgrounds will be split into multiple 256x256 smaller backgrounds automatically, but keep in
mind that VRAM in the GBA is very limited, and that 4 backgrounds will be displayed at a time when
splitting, so the ~1000 tile limit still applies to the combined total of all visible backgrounds
(+ any additional sprite or actor present in your scene).
:::

## Adding a Player

You can now set your scene type to `Top Down` (or whatever grinds your gears) to automatically add
a player.
You'll notice that an arrow has appeared: this is your player, with its default sprite.

<div className="flex flex-col xl:flex-row items-center gap-12">
  <img src="/images/player-arrow.png" alt="Player Default Sprite" className="rounded-xl" height={300} />
  <img src="/images/player-props.png" alt="Player Properties" className="rounded-xl" height={300} />
</div>

<br /><br />

Now that your player exists, you can import a sprite by selecting `File > Import > Sprite` from the
menu (or going to the `Images` tab and clicking the `+` button right to `Sprites`) and choosing an
image file from your computer.

:::tip
If you don't have a sprite on hand, you can download [this one](/images/player.bmp) I used for one
of my own games
(courtesy of [The Pixel Nook](https://the-pixel-nook.itch.io/retro-rpg-character-pack)).
:::

Sprites also have a few requirements (see the [Sprites documentation](/docs/content/sprites) for
more details):
- They must be multiples of 8px, 16px or 32px in either width or height (ex: if you want a 16x16
sprite and have 10 frames horizontally, it should be 160x16px)
- They need a transparency color (usually green #00FF00).

<img src="/images/sprite-import.png" alt="Import Sprite" className="rounded-xl" height={500} />

<br /><br />
Now that you've imported your sprite, you can assign it to your player through the Right Sidebar
under the sprite property.

Run your game to see your player with the newly assigned sprite in action:

  <source src="/videos/player-preview.mp4" type="video/mp4" />

</video>

<br /><br />
:::info
As you can see, it doesn't have animations yet, but you can easily set them up later in the `Images`
tab and the `Animations Editor` panel. See the [Sprites documentation](/docs/content/sprites) for
more details.
:::

## Adding Collisions

Now that you have a colorful scene and a player, you can start adding collisions to your scene.
Collisions define which parts of your scene the player will be blocked by (e.g walls, ground or
obstacles).

Start by selecting the `Collisions` brush in the toolbar (or use the `C` shortcut), and left click
on any part of your scene where you want to add a collision block. This will add a semi-transparent
red square indicating the collision area (add as many as you want):

<img src="/images/collisions.png" alt="Collisions Preview" className="rounded-xl" height={400} />

<br /><br />

:::tip
Collision painting is very easy: left click to add collisions and right click to remove them.
See the [Collisions documentation](/docs/content/collisions) for more details.
:::

Run your game again to discover how collisions affect your player's movements:

  <source src="/videos/player-collisions.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
  Your browser does not support the video tag.
</video>

## Adding Actors

Actors are characters or objects in your scene that your player can interact with.
To add an actor, select the `Add` tool, select `Actor` and click anywhere on your scene.

<img src="/images/add-actor.png" alt="Add an Actor" className="rounded-xl" height={400} />

<br /><br />

You can then assign a sprite, set its position, and configure its properties.

<img src="/images/actor-props.png" alt="Actor Properties" className="rounded-xl" height={500} />

<br /><br />
For this tutorial, we'll add a simple `Show Dialog` event to our actor, in its `On Interact` events
tab in the Right Sidebar.

<img src="/images/actor-dialog.png" alt="Actor Show Dialog Event" className="rounded-xl" height={500} />

<br /><br />

Aaaaand run your game again to see it come to life (press `A` in front of your actor to interact):

<video controls className="rounded-xl" height={400}>
  <source src="/videos/actor-dialog.mp4" type="video/mp4" />

:::info
If an actor's sprite has enough frames to detect directional movement, it will automatically face
the correct direction when the player interacts with it (this can be disabled).
:::

## Adding Sensors

Sensors are invisible areas in your scene that can trigger events when the player enters/exits or
interacts with them.

To add a sensor, select the `Add` tool, select `Sensor` and click anywhere on your scene. It will be
represented by a semi-transparent orange square/rectangle.
You can then resize and position the sensor as needed, and configure its events in the Right
Sidebar.

<img src="/images/add-sensor.png" alt="Add a Sensor" className="rounded-xl" height={400} />

<br /><br />

For this tutorial, we'll be adding `Fade Out` & `Go To Scene` events to our sensor to showcase a
scene transition when the player enters the sensor.

<img src="/images/sensor-events.png" alt="Sensor Events" className="rounded-xl" height={500} />

Inside the `Go To Scene` event, select the same scene you are working on, and just set a different
starting point to see the difference.

Then run your game again to see the scene transition in action.
